## 1. The four intents behind this checklist

People searching for property documents are not one audience. They are four, at four different moments, and they need four different things from the page. This is the single most important finding in this document, because it determines page structure.

| Intent | Moment | What they type | What they need on the page | Commercial value |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Informational, early** | Thinking about buying. Nothing chosen. | "documents required for buying property in India" | A complete list they can save | Low per visit, high in aggregate. Feeds the top of the funnel. |
| **Verification, active** | Property shortlisted. Seller has sent papers. | "is allotment letter enough to buy plot", "how to check if plot is disputed" | A specific yes or no, fast | **Highest.** This person has money in motion. |
| **Panic, mid-transaction** | Something has already gone wrong. | "builder giving possession without OC", "seller not paying property tax what to do" | Reassurance plus a next step | **Highest.** Converts to consultation at the best rate. |
| **Procedural, local** | Knows what they need, does not know where to get it | "how to get fard online Punjab", "GMADA transfer process" | Office name, portal link, fee, timeline | Medium. High local relevance and repeat visits. |

**Implication for the page:** the pillar must serve all four or it serves none well. The current draft does this by opening with the complete list for intent one, using a scannable table for intent two, dedicating the case studies and red flags section to intent three, and giving offices, fees, and timelines for intent four.

**Implication for the cluster:** intents two and three deserve their own dedicated pages. They convert. The pillar captures them and hands them off.

The head terms. Highly competitive, dominated by developer blogs, NBFCs, and banks.

- list of documents to check before buying property
- documents needed to buy a house in India

**Competitive read:** the top 10 is banks (Bajaj, Kotak, Ujjivan), developers (Puravankara, Charan Group, CRA), and legal firms. All of them are generic. None of them name a specific office, a specific fee, or a specific timeline. None of them describe a failure they personally handled.

**Where RHMC wins:** specificity and lived experience. A page that says "GMADA basic transfer costs ₹4,000 to ₹5,000, takes four to eight weeks, and the OTP goes to the last registered buyer" outranks a page that says "ensure all necessary approvals are in place" on every quality signal Google and the AI engines measure.

**Realistic outcome:** page 2 to 3 nationally at first. This cluster is a long game and mostly earns its keep by feeding AI citations, not by ranking first.

## 3. Question phrasings for AEO

AI engines match on question form. These are the exact phrasings to use as H2 and H3 headings, and to answer in 40 to 60 words each.

**Definitional**
- What documents are required to buy property in India?
- What is the most important document when buying property?
- What is the difference between fard and jamabandi?
- What is intkal in Punjab property?
- Is mutation the same as registration?

**Procedural**
- How do I check land records online in Punjab?
- How do I get a non-encumbrance certificate in Punjab?
- How long does a GMADA property transfer take?
- What is the stamp duty in Punjab in 2026?
- How do I verify a builder's RERA registration in Punjab?

**Conditional**
- Can a builder give possession without an occupancy certificate?
- Do I need an encumbrance certificate if I am not taking a loan?
- Can an NRI buy agricultural land in Punjab?
- Is an allotment letter enough to prove ownership?
- What happens if mutation is not done after registry?

**Comparative**
- Sale deed vs conveyance deed: what is the difference?
- Occupancy certificate vs completion certificate?
- Agreement to sell vs sale deed?
- Stamp duty in Punjab vs Chandigarh?

**Rule of thumb:** if a heading is a question, the paragraph immediately under it must be a complete, standalone answer. No lead-in. No "let us understand." An AI engine lifts the paragraph, not the section.

## 4. What the competition is missing

Reviewed the current top-ranking pages for the core term. Every one of them shares the same four gaps.

**Gap 1: no local vocabulary.** Every page is written for a South Indian or Mumbai reader. Khata, patta, 7/12. None of them say fard, jamabandi, intkal, khewat, or khasra. A Punjab buyer reading them is translating in their head.

**Gap 2: no offices, fees, or timelines.** "Obtain the encumbrance certificate." From where. Costing what. Taking how long. Not one page answers this.

**Gap 3: no failure narrative.** Every page describes the ideal process. None describes what it looks like when the process breaks, which is the only version the reader is actually worried about.

**Gap 4: no author.** Most are unsigned or signed by a content team. Google's helpful content system and every AI engine's citation logic both weight demonstrable first-hand experience. An unsigned bank blog cannot demonstrate it. A named advisor who has closed 180+ transactions and navigated five regulatory bodies can.

These four gaps are, in order, RHMC's four advantages. The content plan is simply to fill them.

## 6. Metrics to watch

Traditional rank tracking will under-report this work, because a growing share of these queries are answered without a click.

Track:

- **AI citation appearances.** Query ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and Google AI Mode monthly with the 20 question phrasings in section 3. Log whether realtyconsultants.in is cited. This is the real scoreboard.
- **Impressions, not just clicks,** in Search Console for the question phrasings. Impressions rising while CTR falls is normal and is not failure.
- **Branded search volume** for "Realty Holding Management Consultants," "RHMC Mohali," and "Amritpal Singh property Mohali." Entity recognition shows up here first.
- **WhatsApp enquiries citing the blog.** Add a single qualification question at intake: "How did you find us?" This is the only number that matters commercially.
- **Cluster E page to consultation rate,** tracked separately from the rest. Expect it to be several times higher than the pillar.
